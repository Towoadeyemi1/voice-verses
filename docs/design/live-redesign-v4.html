    It fits the screen by itself

    There is no Laptop or 45″ switch. The app measures its window and picks a layout, and changes it the moment you resize or move to another monitor. Try resizing this window; the size in the dark bar below updates. The projector, lower third and stage outputs already match their own screens' shapes (Settings → Screens → "Match the screen", in 1.3.7).

    Window widthWhat you get
    Under 1000 pxOne column: what is being said, then the screens. For a small laptop or a split screen.
    1000–1900 pxTwo columns: what is being said, and the screens. The other screens are small chips. MacBooks, 24″ and 27″ monitors.
    1900 px and upThe library opens as a column, the screens grow, and the stream, stage and lobby show as live pictures. 34″–49″ ultrawides, 5K iMacs.

    What changed from design 3

    One pattern for everything

    Scripture, songs and the countdown all work the same way: what is on now, large, with what comes next faded below it. Learn it once.

    Calmer colour

    The service parts are soft tints instead of nine loud blocks. Only the part happening now is solid. Strong colour is kept for meaning: red is live, green is next, and the colour team marks what you can press.

    One status, not six buttons

    The top bar has one capsule that says what the app is doing ("Listening · following John 5", or "Following the singers") with Auto or Ask first beside it. Song mode switches itself when singing starts.

    One suggestion at a time

    Instead of three cards, the best guess appears in one quiet bar with how sure it is, and only when there is one. "Use" puts it in Next and Take sends it.

    The Take button in your colour

    Take is the only filled button on the screen, in the colour team's colour, so the next step is never in doubt. Space does the same.

    Fewer labels, bigger words

    No small capitals everywhere. Four text sizes. The verse can be as large as 60 px on a big monitor, and the word being read is underlined in your colour.
