    What the best designs taught, and what design 3 takes from each

    I looked at the church presentation apps, the pro apps people admire most, and the broadcast switchers operators already trust under pressure. Each finding became one decision.

    ProPresenter

    Power that volunteers find hard

    Its toolbar switches between Show, Edit, Reflow, Bible and a menu of five more editors. It has seven separate Clear buttons, one per layer. Reviewers describe a steep curve for volunteers, and one long-time user says they never touched more than a quarter of it.

    Design 3: one screen, no modes. One Clear (Esc), with the layers inside it for the rare time you need them.

    ATEM, vMix

    Red is on air, green is next

    Every switcher uses this, and operators already read it without thinking. vMix users report missing it when only a thin header carries the colour, so they ask for full borders.

    Design 3: a full red frame around On Air and a full green frame around Next, plus the words. One Take button sits between them, as on a switcher.

    Linear, 2026 refresh

    Navigation recedes, the work leads

    Fewer and softer borders ("structure should be felt, not seen"), warmer greys instead of cold blue ones, and a dimmed sidebar so the main area stands out.

    Design 3: almost no boxes. Space and light separate areas, the library is a quiet icon rail, and the greys are warm graphite.

    Final Cut Pro, Logic

    The viewer is the hero; regions stay put

    Apple's pro apps fit everything on one screen in fixed places, and you show or hide areas instead of dragging windows around.

    Design 3: fixed regions with the same thing in the same place every Sunday. On a 45″ screen the library opens as a column and the screens grow.

    Liquid Glass, macOS Tahoe

    Glass for what floats, solid for what you read

    Apple's new material puts content first. Reviewers found text gets lost when glass sits over busy backgrounds.

    Design 3: glass only on the toolbar and the ⌘K palette. Reading surfaces are solid, and the live slide's colours light the window softly from behind.

    FreeShow, EasyWorship

    Clean, but you have to know what to do next

    Reviewers like FreeShow's clean layout but find it less guided.

    Design 3: Next is always filled with the app's best guess, so the only decision is Take or not. The assistant lane shows what it heard and how sure it is.
